You are evaluating whether a generated tribal knowledge book contains one gold atomic claim.

Gold evidence explains why the gold claim is true.
Do NOT count gold evidence as evidence in the book.
Only score what the book itself says.

claim_text states the claim in full and defines its meaning.
Every claim has a main finding: the specific assertion the claim exists to establish. It is what the match.must_include points jointly state, anchored in the first sentence of claim_text. The main finding is not the claim's topic or headline. A book that only says the topic exists, or gives a generic description of it, has not stated the finding.
match.must_include lists the points to look for in the book. A point counts when its meaning is in the book, as defined below; exact wording is not required. Details that appear in claim_text but not in must_include (extra figures, definitions of terms, resumption dates, secondary numbers) are not required and must not be used to downgrade a point or the main finding.
Use match.must_not_include to detect contradictions. A forbidden point is found only when the book asserts its meaning, that is, the opposite of what the claim says. Compare meaning, not keywords. A book that omits a detail, describes a neutral default or fallback, or gives a general formula next to documented exceptions or warnings does not assert a forbidden point.

A must_include point is found when its meaning is present in the book, in any wording:
- Each distinct element a point names (a count, a value, a cause, a date, a fix or its behavior, "never refreshed", "no reader", "verified in the log") must be stated or clearly entailed by the book's own words. A generic mention of the topic, an adjacent fact, or a neighbouring source does not carry a specific element. A fact about a different version, job, table or period does not carry the point. Do not invent a link the book does not make.
- Judge by what the book's facts amount to, not by its labels. A different name for the same state or fact counts, and so does a consequence that follows directly from the book's own statements. An absence that the book shows from several angles (no definition, no writer, none in the data) carries a point about something not existing. Do not demand a mechanism, procedure or source that the point itself does not name.
- A point that joins several facts is found when the book states each of its elements. A half-met point is not found when the missing half is a separate fact the point names. It is found only when the missing half is a mere restatement or precision of what the book says.
- A numeric point is found when the book gives the same quantity and the same rule behind it, even if the value is shifted or stale. It is not found when the number implies a different rule or threshold, or when the quantity is absent.
- The parts of a point may sit in different places in the book. Read the book as a whole and combine them; a point is found when the pieces together state its meaning, wherever they appear.
- A point about a formula or rule is found when the book expresses the same relationship with all of its parts, such as coefficients, weights or thresholds, in words or in any other form. The exact formula is not required.
- A negative point ("not an experiment", "no overlap", "read by nothing") is found when the book's stated facts entail it, for example a single switch date with the old version retired and no parallel arm described. It is not found when the book only gives adjacent facts that leave it open.

Main finding grading follows the points:
- "full" only when every must_include point is found. Do not call it "full" because the book covers the topic or the first-sentence headline while a point naming a separate fact (a figure, a cause, a date, a verification source, a stated behavior) is not found. A missing point that merely restates or sharpens what the book already says does not block "full".
- "part" when at least one point is found, or the book states the finding only in part or without the specifics that identify it, but some point naming a separate fact is not found.
- "none" when no point is found and the book does not state the first-sentence finding with its specifics.

Work in this order:
1. Read the whole book first. List every passage that touches the topic of the claim: quote each passage and name the section it comes from. The passages that matter are often spread across the book.
2. State the claim's main finding in one sentence, with its identifying specifics.
3. For each must_include point, in the order given, decide from those passages whether its meaning is present. Quote the passage or passages that carry it; if nothing in the book carries it, write "none" and set found to false. Do the same for each must_not_include point.
4. Decide whether the book states the main finding: "full" when the book states it with its identifying specifics, "part" when the book states it only in part or without the specifics that identify it, "none" when the book does not state it, even if it states other must_include points.
5. The most common error on this task is calling a point not found when the book does state it, in other words or in another place. Before you set found to false for any point, search the book once more for it.

The verdict is computed from your decisions: contradicted if any must_not_include point is found; otherwise correct when the main finding is "full", even if a must_include point that explains, refines or quantifies it is not found; partial when the main finding is "part"; missing when the main finding is "none". Fill in verdict with your own call as well.
